# Monte Carlo Simulation

Real race outcomes are not deterministic. A driver can lose time behind traffic, a pit stop can take longer than average, and a safety car can invalidate a carefully timed strategy window. The simulation accounts for this uncertainty by running many versions of the same strategy rather than producing a single number.

**How it works in this project:**

1. **Strategy candidates** are generated by enumerating 1-stop and 2-stop combinations across all available dry compounds. Every generated or custom strategy must cover the exact race distance, keep every stint within the configured limits, use available compounds, and satisfy the two-dry-compound rule unless explicitly disabled.

2. **Base lap-time predictions** are produced by the trained HGB or Ridge model for each lap in the strategy, given the compound, tyre life, stint number, weather conditions, and other features.

3. **Correlated residual noise** is sampled from the empirical residuals stored in `predictions_{model}.parquet`. Five-lap moving blocks are drawn from historical driver/stint sequences, with compound-specific blocks where available. This retains short-run error correlation instead of treating every lap as independent.

4. **Pit-stop time cost** is estimated against clean laps close to each stop rather than a driver's full-race median, reducing fuel-load and track-evolution bias. Fixed mode uses the displayed race median; sample mode draws around that median using the measured spread.

5. **Total race time** = sum of all predicted lap times + sum of all pit-stop time costs. This is computed for every simulation run. The distribution of totals gives P10/P50/P90 outcome bands for each strategy.

6. **Counterfactual support** is checked against prior-season tyre-life distributions for the circuit and compound. Laps beyond the 99th-percentile support limit receive a conservative penalty; global history is used only when circuit-specific support is unavailable.

7. **Strategies are ranked** by a conservative score: mean race time + 25% of the P90 upper-tail margin + historical-support penalty. Mean and P10/P50/P90 remain visible so the ranking is auditable.

The number of simulations is configurable from 1 (deterministic, fixed pit loss) up to 2,000.
